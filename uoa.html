UOA flags

flow viewer positioning map pre-open brief
Context, not a signal. A flag is one contract's burst within ~5 minutes: ≥ 1,000 contracts and ≥ $100k, ≥ 70% at the ask (bought) or the bid (sold), mostly single-leg, at least the size of the open interest it started the day with, 0-10% out of the money, ≤ 21 days. Side and opening/closing are inferred from where trades printed. Next-day OI is the next session's start-of-day open interest minus the flag day's, against all the contract's flagged volume that day: opened / written ≥ 50%, partly 10-50%, didn't stick, closing ≤ −10%. Since: from the flag's average price, the best price since for the flag's side (the high for a buyer, the low for a writer; green = in that side's favour) and the latest price. ER: an earnings report falls before the expiry -- the premium is largely the event.
Our tests (check_uoa_paths, 2023-10..2026-09, 16k ETF and 9k stock flags): bought right after the burst, at the next open once OI confirmed, or an hour in, these contracts reached +100 / +150 / +200 / +300% exactly as often as unprinted contracts of the same profile, and slightly less often than their same-day unprinted twins; stuck prints predicted nothing over 1-5 days (check_uoa_events). A forward test on new data is scheduled for ~2027-04. Live flags diff UW's contract rows between passes; backfilled days use the lake's minute bars in the same 5-minute buckets.
Whole market · EOD: once after the close, UW's hottest-chains screener with the whole-day version of the flag (day volume ≥ 1,000 and above the day's starting open interest, ≥ $100k, ≥ 70% one side, mostly single-leg, 0-10% out of the money, ≤ 21 days) across every optionable US ticker. Whole-day totals, not a 5-minute burst, so it is looser and slower than the live flags. On backfilled days only the NEXT earnings date was known.
